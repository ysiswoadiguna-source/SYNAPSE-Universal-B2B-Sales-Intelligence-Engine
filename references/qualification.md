## 1. The evidence ledger
Before any judgment, sort what you have into four columns. Doing this on the page, not in your head, is what stops a hopeful sentence from becoming a forecast.

| Label | What belongs here | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Fact | Something documented or done: a PO, a drawing sent, a site visit, a dated message | "PO no. 4512 diterima 3 Nov" |
| Customer claim | Something the customer said that we haven't verified | "Budget sudah ada" |
| Inference | Your reading of facts and claims | "Purchasing asking about stock suggests the user already chose us" |
| Unknown | What nobody has said | Who signs, when they decide |

Two rules. A claim repeated three times is still a claim. And an inference never appears in customer text as if it were known.

## 2. The seven qualifiers
For each one, note whether it is evidenced, only claimed, or unknown. The "cheapest question" is the lowest-pressure way to find out — use one per message, never the whole list.

| Qualifier | Counts as evidence | Does not count | Cheapest question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Need | A described problem, a breakdown, a project with a name, a replacement that is due | "Mau tanya harga saja" | "Ini untuk penggantian unit yang ada atau proyek baru Pak?" |
| Spec | Drawing, nameplate, datasheet, a written requirement list | A product name with no parameters | "Boleh minta foto nameplate atau gambarnya Pak, biar tidak salah tawar?" |
| Access | We have spoken with the user or someone who decides | A forwarded request from a third party | "Untuk detail teknisnya, lebih enak saya koordinasi dengan siapa Pak?" |
| Budget | A stated range, an approved budget line, a comparison against a known price | "Budget ada" with no figure | "Kisaran budgetnya di angka berapa Pak, supaya saya sesuaikan opsinya?" |
| Dated trigger | A shutdown date, a project milestone, a fiscal deadline, a tender schedule | "Secepatnya", "segera" | "Barangnya dibutuhkan di lokasi kapan Pak?" |
| Decision process | Named approvers, steps, and paperwork (PR, PO, vendor registration) | "Nanti diajukan" | "Setelah penawaran, prosesnya ke siapa dulu Pak sebelum PO?" |
| Alternative | Named competitor, repair option, or a stated reason to do nothing | Silence about alternatives | "Selain opsi ini, ada alternatif lain yang sedang dipertimbangkan Pak?" |

The status quo is always an alternative. If the customer can keep running as they are for another year at little cost, that is the competitor to beat, and the dated trigger is usually what beats it.

## 3. From evidence to a probability
1. **Pick the stage anchor** from the core table (5-15, 20-35, 40-60, 65-80, 85-95) using what the customer has done, not what they have said they will do.
2. **Place the number inside the band** by counting evidenced qualifiers. Few evidenced: bottom of the band. Most evidenced: top.
3. **Apply the caps.** No contact with a user or decider: 25% at most. No date of any kind: 35% at most. A deal that has slipped its own stated date twice with no new reason: drop one band.
4. **State confidence separately.** 0-2 qualifiers evidenced: low. 3-4: medium. 5 or more: high. Low confidence with a high number is a warning to the user, so say it plainly.
5. **Name the key unknown** — the single missing qualifier whose answer would move the number most — and the next signal that would resolve it.

Give a range when the evidence is thin ("20-30%, confidence rendah") rather than a falsely precise figure. Do not move the number because the customer is friendly: warmth is relationship quality, which is tracked separately.

Signals that raise the read: the customer asks about delivery, receiving, payment documents, or vendor registration; a second person from their side joins; they send information without being asked. Signals that lower it: requests come only through an intermediary; questions are only about price; dates pass without comment; they ask for a re-quote with no change in scope.

## 4. Calibrating against real outcomes
The anchors and caps here are reasoned starting values. They have not been measured against this user's deals. Whenever the user shares outcomes, use them:
- If they say "deals like this usually close", ask how many of the last ten did, and use that rate as the anchor.
- If they keep a record of past reads and results, compare: of the deals called 60-70%, did roughly two in three close? If far fewer did, lower that band and tell the user you are doing so.
- Prefer the user's history for their product and customer type over any general table.

## 5. Mapping the buying center
List people by role, not title. One person may hold several roles; a role may be empty.

| Role | What they care about | How to recognise them |
|---|---|---|
| User | That it works, is safe, and is easy to live with | Describes the problem in operating terms |
| Technical evaluator | Spec compliance, compatibility | Asks for datasheets, drawings, standards |
| Champion | Wants us to win and will act for it | Shares internal information, warns us of problems |
| Economic buyer | Return, risk, timing | Rarely in the chat; is referred to ("atasan", "manajemen") |
| Budget owner | Whether money exists this period | Mentions budget year, CAPEX/OPEX |
| Purchasing | Price, terms, documents, process | Asks for best price, TOP, validity, vendor documents |
| Gatekeeper | Controls access | Forwards, filters, schedules |
| Blocker | Prefers another option or no change | Raises new objections after old ones are resolved |
| Trader / intermediary | Their own margin and speed | Cannot answer technical questions |

A champion is tested by action, not friendliness: have they done something for us that cost them effort? For any deal worth a plan, name which roles we have reached and the one we most need next. Single-threaded deals — one contact, however warm — are fragile; say so.

## 6. Scenario analysis
Use when the evidence supports more than one story. For each scenario give: evidence for, evidence against, rough probability, and the next signal that would confirm or kill it.

Standard set to choose from: active buying · internal delay (approval, budget cycle) · competitor-led (we are the comparison quote) · budget block · no decision (status quo wins) · already lost.

Rules: three to five scenarios; probabilities sum to about 100%; the deal-close probability you report must be consistent with them; end with the one action that produces the most information across scenarios — usually a single easy question, not a push.

## 7. Parking and disqualifying
Park (pause effort, keep the door open) when: two touches went unanswered and nothing has changed; the timeline is real but distant; the request came through layers with no access.
Disqualify (stop investing) when: there is no real need; it is price harvesting; requirements cannot be met; effort is far out of proportion to value.

Either way, the last message is short, kind, and leaves a return path:
> "Baik Pak, kami standby dulu ya. Kalau nanti kebutuhannya sudah jalan, kabari saja, penawarannya saya update."

Tell the user what would reopen it (a date, a spec, an introduction) so parking is a decision with a trigger, not neglect.

## 8. Analyze-mode output template
Keep it tight; a diagnosis nobody reads changes nothing.

```
[Customer] — [what is happening, one line]
Stage: ...            Domain: ...
Objective (theirs): ...
Evidence: Fact ... | Claim ... | Inference ... | Unknown ...
Buying center: reached ... / missing ...
Signals for: ...      Risks / objections: ...
Alternatives (incl. no change): ...
Probability: ..%  ·  Confidence: low/medium/high  ·  Key unknown: ...
Scenarios (if uncertain): A ..% / B ..% / C ..% — next signal: ...
Next best action: ... (why this, why now)
Draft / talking points: ...
```

For meeting prep, replace the draft with: the one thing to learn, the one thing to get agreed (from the commitment ladder), the likely objection and your answer, and what you will not concede in the room.
