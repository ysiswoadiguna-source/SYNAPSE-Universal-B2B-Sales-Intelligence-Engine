Read this when the short playbook in SKILL.md isn't enough. Each entry gives the aim, the move, what to avoid, and an example line. Example lines are patterns to adapt, not scripts — rotate the wording.

## 1. New inquiry
**Aim:** keep the customer talking and learn what the offer depends on.
**Move:** reply quickly; answer whatever can be answered now; ask for at most two details, chosen because the offer would be wrong without them. Offer a call only if the need is complex.
**Avoid:** a long questionnaire; a formal quotation built on guesses; asking for budget in the first message.
> "Siap Pak, bisa kami bantu. Untuk kapasitas dan tegangan listriknya berapa ya Pak? Biar penawarannya langsung pas."

## 2. Quote sent, then silence
Silence after a quotation is normal: the customer is usually waiting on someone else. The cadence below works because each touch brings something new, so the customer has a reason to answer.

| Touch | When | What it adds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A few working days after the quote, or near any date they mentioned | A check that the quote was received and is complete; an offer to clarify one specific point |
| 2 | About a week after touch 1 | New information: stock or price-validity status the user has verified, a technical clarification, an alternative option |
| 3 | Only if something has changed | A decision-window question with a graceful exit |
| Then | — | Nurture or park. No fourth chase. |

Timing is a guide; the customer's own stated dates always override it. If the user has nothing new to add for touch 2, the honest advice is to wait.
> "Pak Andi, penawaran kemarin ada bagian yang perlu saya jelaskan atau sesuaikan? Kalau masih proses internal, tidak apa-apa Pak."

## 3. Intermediaries and traders
**Aim:** get the real specification or an introduction, without threatening the intermediary's position.
**Move:** one request for the original spec, drawing, nameplate, or the end user's contact, framed as avoiding a wrong offer. Give indicative information meanwhile. If the intermediary cannot provide it, give a short window and park.
**Avoid:** going around them; investing in detailed engineering for an unknown end user; treating their enthusiasm as demand.
> "Supaya tidak salah tawar Pak, boleh diteruskan foto nameplate atau spek dari user-nya?"

## 4. Tenders and formal procurement
**Aim:** be compliant and present at each stage; spend effort where the calendar says it counts.
**Move:** ask which stage they are in and the next date (clarification, submission, evaluation, award). Offer the documents the stage needs. Confirm in writing anything said verbally. For consortium or joint-operation bids, ask who the commercial decision sits with.
**Avoid:** chasing between stages; asking "kapan closing"; informal price changes outside the process.
> "Untuk tender ini sekarang di tahap apa Pak, dan tanggal berikutnya kapan? Dokumen pendukung yang dibutuhkan bisa kami siapkan."

## 5. Purchasing-led contact
**Aim:** make it easy for purchasing to finish their check.
**Move:** answer exactly what was asked — price, stock, delivery, validity, documents — cleanly and fast, using only what the user has verified. If they say it is going to management, ask once for a decision window and then wait.
**Avoid:** re-selling the product to someone verifying it; volunteering a discount; long explanations.

## 6. Competitor-led deal
Signs that we are the comparison quote: the specification matches another brand's wording, the deadline is very short, questions are only about price, there is no access to the user.
**Move:** ask one question that only a serious buyer would answer (the application, the site condition, the decision date). If it is answered, compete on scope and total cost. If it isn't, give a correct but minimal offer and limit effort.
**Avoid:** discounting to "stay in the game"; criticising the competitor.

## 7. Postponed or budget freeze
**Aim:** convert an open-ended delay into a dated one.
**Move:** accept the delay without pressure; ask when it will be reviewed; offer to keep the offer current. Record the date and park until then.
> "Tidak apa-apa Pak. Kira-kira dibahas lagi di bulan apa? Nanti penawarannya saya update mendekati waktunya."

## 8. PO received and order confirmation
Same day: thank them, restate the PO number and what was ordered, give delivery as an estimate, ask for the receiving contact and hours, and flag any difference between the PO and the quotation (price, quantity, terms, specification) before processing. A mismatch found now is a clarification; found at delivery it is a dispute.

## 9. Delivery delay and other bad news
**Aim:** the customer hears it from us first, with what happens next.
**Move:** say what changed, the reason in one line, the new estimate (as an estimate, verified by the user), and what we are doing about it. Apologise once. Offer an option if one exists (partial delivery, substitute, loan unit).
**Avoid:** vague wording, blaming a third party at length, a new date the user hasn't confirmed.
> "Pak Rudi, mohon maaf, pengiriman mundur dari jadwal karena [alasan]. Estimasi baru [tanggal]. Kalau ada kebutuhan mendesak, kabari ya Pak, kami carikan opsinya."

## 10. Payment follow-up
**Aim:** find where the invoice is stuck and help move it.
**Move:** ask which stage it is in (received, verified, approved, scheduled) and whether any document is missing; offer to resend the invoice, tax invoice, or delivery note. Keep the tone the same as before the sale.
**Avoid:** repeating "kapan dibayar"; implying bad faith; involving people outside finance without the user's decision.
> "Bu Sari, untuk invoice no. [...] sekarang posisinya sudah di tahap apa ya? Kalau ada dokumen yang kurang, saya kirim ulang."

## 11. Service complaint
Structure the reply: what happened and the cause as far as it is known → what has been done → what is a wear part or consumable and so is replaced, not repaired → what we propose and when → any operating restriction until it is fixed. If the cause is not yet known, say that and give the time of the next update. Never minimise anything related to safety or protection devices.

## 12. Price change or expired validity
Tell the customer before they issue a PO on the old price. State the new price or that it is being rechecked, the reason in one line, and — if the user has confirmed it — until when the old price can still be honoured. A real cutoff date is useful information; an invented one is fake urgency.

## 13. Internal handover
One short message introducing the new owner by name, saying they have the history, and what the customer should expect next. The new owner verifies price, stock, and terms before confirming anything the previous owner said.

## 14. Lost deal and reactivation
**After a loss:** one question about what decided it, thanks, door open. The answer is the most valuable calibration data the user will get — suggest they record it.
**Reactivation:** needs a reason the customer would recognise: a new requirement, a changed price or stock position, an end-of-warranty or inspection date, a new product that removes the old objection.
> "Terima kasih infonya Pak. Kalau boleh tahu, pertimbangan utamanya di harga, waktu, atau spesifikasinya? Untuk bahan evaluasi kami."

## 15. Existing customer: repeat and expansion
Start from what they already own and use. Useful triggers: consumption or wear cycles, a second site or line, an expiring service period, a problem reported by the user. Ask about the operation, not about "kebutuhan lain". A satisfied user is also the easiest route to the people in the buying center we have not met.

## 16. Meeting or call preparation
Give the user one page:
- **Learn:** the single unknown that matters most, and the question that reveals it.
- **Agree:** the next rung on the commitment ladder to ask for.
- **Expect:** the most likely objection and the response.
- **Hold:** what will not be conceded in the room, and what would need approval.
- **Bring:** documents or samples the stage needs.
- **After:** a same-day written summary of what was agreed.
